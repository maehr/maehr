Dr. sc. ETH Zurich Moritz Mähr is a historian of technology. He studies how computers change institutions and everyday life, especially in Swiss public administration and migration governance.

He is a Research Associate in Digital History at the [Department of History](https://www.hist.unibe.ch/) and an Associate Researcher in [Digital Humanities](https://www.dh.unibe.ch/) at the University of Bern. He is also a Lecturer in History at [UniDistance.ch](https://unidistance.ch/en/studies/master/master-of-arts-in-historical-sciences/syllabus#flush-heading-9), an External Lecturer in [Data Design + Art](https://www.hslu.ch/en/lucerne-school-of-design-film-and-art/degree-programmes/bachelor/data-design-and-art/) at HSLU, and a Technical Advisor for the University of Zurich research project [Sexual Abuse in the Catholic Church in Switzerland](https://www.hist.uzh.ch/de/fachbereiche/neuzeit/privatdozierende/meier/forschung/forschungsprojekte/sexueller-missbrauch.html). Until 2025, he was the Project Manager of [Stadt.Geschichte.Basel](https://www.stadtgeschichtebasel.ch/) at the University of Basel and a Visiting Research Fellow at the [Luxembourg Centre for Contemporary and Digital History (C²DH)](https://www.uni.lu/c2dh-en/), University of Luxembourg. In May and June 2026, he was a Visiting Research Fellow at the University of Potsdam, and until 2026 an Information and Library Science Specialist with [Research Analytics Services](https://ethz.ch/staffnet/en/organisation/departments/eth-library/research-support-services/research-analytics-service.html) at ETH Zurich.

He studies the history of computing as a history of institutions, knowledge, and power. In digital history, he examines how digitisation, metadata, and AI shape the sources available to researchers. His current agenda spans five areas:

1. 🧠 **Digital Source Criticism in the Age of AI** – reproducible methods for identifying bias in data, algorithms, and interfaces.
2. 🌐 **Critical AI Studies in Digital Humanities & Digital History** – critical AI literacy, audit studies, dataset ethnography, and bias-aware metadata workflows.
3. 🗃️ **Histories of Digitisation** – the history of digitization in public administration, especially the Swiss migration regime.
4. 🏛️ **Public History Infrastructure** – low-barrier, open-source platforms like _Stadt.Geschichte.Basel_ for FAIR/CARE storytelling and data reuse.
5. 🌱 **Digital Sustainability** – minimal computing, reproducibility, inclusivity, and ethical longevity in scholarly workflows.

Moritz studied history, philosophy of knowledge, computer science, and banking and finance in Zurich and Berlin. He received his doctorate from ETH Zurich for his dissertation on the [digitization of Swiss migration authorities in the 1960s](https://doi.org/10.30965/9783657796823). His work operationalizes Open Science principles in digital history through reproducible methods, interoperable infrastructures, and participatory governance.
